# Pitch material

Owned by the storyteller and the editor. Every factual line must be cleared by the science and data lead against `docs/SCIENCE_BRIEF.md` before it goes on a slide.

## Name and tagline

**Sol Stroll.** Know before you go.

## Problem statement (draft)

> The first astronauts on Mars will have superb maps of where to walk, and nothing that tells them whether today is the day to do it. Sol Stroll joins Curiosity's path, photos, science findings and 14 years of measured conditions into one go / no-go briefing for a single real Marswalk.

Shorter, for a form field:

> Mars maps show where to walk. Sol Stroll tells a crew whether to go: one briefing for one real Marswalk, built from four kinds of NASA data.

Check before use: "14 years" is the span of the weather feed (Aug 2012 to Aug 2026). "Four kinds of data" must match what is actually in the product on Sunday.

## Opening (agreed)

> "You're on Mars. Your science target is four hundred metres away. The ground is flat. The route is clear.
>
> Do you go?
>
> Your map says yes. Your map is looking at a photo taken years ago. It doesn't know what today is like.
>
> Your map tells you where. Sol Stroll tells you when, and when not to."

Notes:
- Pause after "Do you go?".
- "Four hundred metres" is a placeholder. The real route is about 1.2 km from the first stop to the last; the nodule site is about 0.7 km along it.
- "A photo taken years ago" needs the real date of the imagery.
- The last line is the editor's suggested replacement for a list of "when, why, and how". Team to confirm.

## Seven slides

Each slide names the judging criterion it is there to win.

| # | Slide | What it says | Criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Do you go?** | The opening, over the logo. Nothing else on the slide | Presentation |
| 2 | **The problem** | Orbital maps are static. Mars is not: in the 2018 dust storm the daily high at Curiosity's site fell from 4 °C to −29 °C in six weeks. A crew cannot wait 20 minutes for Earth to answer | Impact |
| 3 | **What we built** | One real walk in the Mount Sharp boxwork, answering five questions: why, where, when, how sure, what if. Screenshot of the map | Relevance |
| 4 | **Why this walk** | The boxwork: groundwater that outlasted the lakes. Curiosity needed nine months and four drill holes to cover 1.4 km. A person could cross it in an afternoon | Impact, Creativity |
| 5 | **The evidence** | The data table: which mission, which instrument, what it gives us. Then the honesty: what the data cannot tell us | Validity |
| 6 | **What makes it different** | Every other tool plans the route. Ours gives the verdict, and replays a real bad day against the plan. Screenshot of the storm replay | Creativity |
| 7 | **Know before you go** | What a crew gets, what comes next, the link, the team, the non-endorsement line | Presentation |

Rules for the deck:
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two sentences of explanation, it is two slides or it is cut.
- Slides use the **light theme** (see `DESIGN.md`).
- Every number has its source in small text at the bottom of the slide.
- Slide 5 is where this project beats the field. Do not cut it for time.

## If it is a 30-second video instead

| Seconds | Content |
|---|---|
| 0 to 8 | The opening, shortened: "You're on Mars. Your target is four hundred metres away. The route is clear. Do you go?" |
| 8 to 20 | Screen recording: the map, a stop with orbit and ground photo side by side, the verdict changing as the sol changes |
| 20 to 27 | The storm replay: the verdict turns to no-go |
| 27 to 30 | Logo and tagline |

A 30-second video cannot carry the evidence slide. **The slides are the safer choice for this project**, because its strength is Validity.

## Facts not yet cleared

| Claim | Problem |
|---|---|
| 4 °C to −29 °C in 2018 | From the weather feed, which is labelled outreach-only. Daily maximum air temperature, sols 2060 and 2100 |
| A 20-minute wait for Earth | From memory, not checked. The delay varies with the planets' positions |
| Nine months, four drill holes, 1.4 km | Derived from the waypoint file and the mission blog. See the science brief |
| "A person could cross it in an afternoon" | Our claim. It depends on an assumed walking speed |
| Every other tool plans the route | True of the 12 public entries we read, not of the whole field. Say "the entries we reviewed" |
